Executive Summary Draft2 pages

Many high school students head to college without understanding how tuition, student loans, and budgeting work, and they make decisions that follow them for a decade or more. Centspire is a free, mobile-friendly web game, Swipe Through College, that teaches these three topics. Students make 10 real money decisions from senior year to their first job by swiping cards. Each of the game's four chapters opens with a 30-second interactive lesson, and every choice shows what it costs, the math behind it, and a new money term for the student's Money Notebook.

Our team designed and built the game in three versions, improving engagement each time. Next, financial and technical professionals will review it, and we will promote it through digital channels and an in-person pop-up event. We will measure success with a before-and-after quiz built into the game, website visits, completed games, and replays.

To finish after execution: key results, knowledge improvement, and recommendations.

Scope

  • Purpose: teach high schoolers the basics of tuition, student loans, and budgeting before they make college decisions.
  • Benefits: fewer costly mistakes, more aid claimed (FAFSA), smarter borrowing.
  • Why high schoolers: the biggest money decisions (where to go, how to pay) happen in junior and senior year.
  • How we relate: we're students facing these decisions ourselves…
  • In scope: web game with lessons, decisions, and quizzes; team dashboard for results; digital and in-person outreach at our school.
  • Out of scope (for now): native App Store app, personal financial advice, other schools.

Quality & Risk Management Draft1 page

How we'll ensure the website is accurate, functional, and accessible.

Quality management

  • Accuracy: financial reviewer checks all figures, quiz answers, and lesson content; every figure is cited in the bibliography. All student-facing text is in one file (version-3/content.js) so reviewers can check it without reading code.
  • Functionality: technical reviewer and team test on iPhone, Android, and Chromebook.
  • Usability: student testing with classmates; we watch them use it and note where they get stuck or lose interest.
  • Accessibility: readable text size, color contrast, keyboard navigation (arrow keys swipe), screen-reader announcements, reduced-motion support, works on phones without installing anything.
